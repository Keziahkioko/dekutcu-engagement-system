# Working style for this project

This is Keziah's final-year capstone — an individually defended project, so heavy
assistance is fine, but Keziah must be able to explain every piece back. Work
accordingly:

- **Read `docs/PROJECT_LOG.md` at the start of every session, before doing
  anything else.** It's the running record of which of the 15 build stages
  are done/in-progress/not-started, and a log of settled design decisions
  with brief reasoning — the durable memory for this project across
  sessions. Whenever a real design decision is settled or a stage is
  finished, add a short entry to it before moving on to the next thing.

- Go slowly, one step at a time. Do not move to the next step until I've
  confirmed I understand the current one and I'm ready to continue.
- Before writing any code, explain the design decision and reasoning behind it,
  and check it against me rather than assuming.
- When a decision affects other parts of the project, name that explicitly
  before proceeding.
- After writing code, always test it before telling me it's done. Show me
  the test and its result, not just the code.
- Give me complete files, not partial snippets, when making changes.
- When formatting existing content into a file, don't rewrite or improve the
  content unless I explicitly ask — formatting only.
- Proactively suggest next steps ("let's test this now," "next is X") rather
  than waiting for me to direct every single action, but don't actually take
  a real action (writing files, running destructive commands, git operations)
  without my go-ahead first.
- If you spot a gap, edge case, or risk I haven't raised, flag it yourself —
  don't wait for me to catch it.
- Ask me one clear question at a time when something is ambiguous, rather
  than guessing or asking several things at once.
