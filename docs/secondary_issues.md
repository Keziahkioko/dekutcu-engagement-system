# Fundamental truths and secondary issues — a guide for DeKUTCU members

> **Approved for DeKUTCU by Keziah Kioko, 2026-10-15** (drafted 2026-10-12; accuracy review 2026-10-15 at
> Keziah's request -- each view checked against how its own tradition states it). Part of the companion's materials
> (tier `dekutcu`), so the bot explains secondary issues fairly instead of declining. Every view is described
> as its own holders would describe it, with no view marked "correct".

## Why this guide exists

DeKUTCU brings together students from many churches — Anglican, Presbyterian, Methodist, Baptist,
Pentecostal, AIC, Catholic background, and others. The constitution calls the Union
"non-denominational" (Preamble, clause 4) and states the truths we hold in common in its doctrinal basis
(Article 11).

Christians have long distinguished between:

- **Fundamental truths** — the heart of the Christian faith. To deny them is to leave historic Christianity.
- **Secondary issues** — questions on which sincere, Bible-believing Christians disagree. They matter, and
  churches often organise their life around them, but they do not decide whether someone is a Christian.

A well-known saying captures the spirit: *"In essentials, unity; in non-essentials, liberty; in all things,
charity."* Some teachers speak of "theological triage": first-order truths that define the faith,
second-order issues that may lead Christians to belong to different churches, and third-order questions
on which Christians can differ while worshipping side by side.

## The fundamental truths DeKUTCU holds (Article 11)

These are the truths every member of the Union shares:

- **(A)** One God existing as three Divine Persons — Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
- **(B)** The sovereignty of God in creation, revelation, redemption and final judgment.
- **(C)** The divine inspiration and entire trustworthiness of the Holy Scriptures, and their supreme
  authority in all matters of faith and conduct.
- **(D)** Redemption from sin solely through the sacrificial, substitutionary death of the Lord Jesus Christ.
- **(E)** The bodily resurrection of Jesus Christ and His ascension.
- **(F)** Justification of the sinner by the grace of God alone, through faith.
- **(G)** The indwelling and work of the Holy Spirit in the believer.
- **(H)** The progressive sanctification of the believer.
- **(I)** The expectation of the second coming of the Lord Jesus Christ.
- **(J)** The one holy universal Church, the body of Christ, to which all believers belong.
- **(K)** The unity of believers in Christ without discrimination of any kind.

## Secondary issues — where DeKUTCU does not take a side

On each issue below, DeKUTCU does not take a position. Members are encouraged to study the Scriptures,
to follow the teaching of their own church, and to discuss these questions with grace.

### Baptism

**What all agree on:** Jesus commanded His followers to baptise "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of
the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19). Baptism marks entry into Christ's church, and it is connected with faith in
Christ, cleansing from sin and union with Him (Acts 2:38; Romans 6:3-4).

**What does baptism do?**
- **A sacrament through which God gives grace** — God uses baptism to unite a person to Christ and to give the
  new birth and forgiveness it signifies, received by faith. Catholic and Orthodox teaching; Lutherans hold
  that God works through baptism by His promise; the Anglican Articles call it a sign of the new birth by which,
  "as by an instrument", believers are joined to the church.
- **An ordinance that pictures what God has done** — baptism is an act of obedience that publicly shows the
  cleansing and new life the believer has already received by faith; it does not itself bring the new birth.
  Held by Baptist, Pentecostal and many evangelical and independent churches.

**Who should be baptised?**
- **Believer's baptism (credobaptism).** Baptism is for those who have personally confessed faith in
  Christ. In the New Testament, people believe and are then baptised (Acts 2:41; Acts 8:12; Acts 8:36-38).
  Held by Baptist, Pentecostal and many independent churches.
- **Infant baptism (paedobaptism).** Children of believers are baptised as members of the covenant
  community, as circumcision was given to infants in the Old Testament (Genesis 17; Colossians 2:11-12).
  Whole households were baptised (Acts 16:15; Acts 16:33). Faith is to be confessed as the child grows.
  Held by Anglican, Presbyterian, Methodist, Lutheran, Catholic and Orthodox churches.

**How should baptism be done?**
- **By immersion only** — the word "baptise" means to dip or immerse, and going under the water pictures
  burial and resurrection with Christ (Romans 6:3-4). Held by Baptist and many Pentecostal churches.
- **Immersion, pouring or sprinkling** — the meaning lies in the washing, not the amount of water; pouring
  pictures the Spirit poured out (Acts 2:17), and sprinkling recalls cleansing (Ezekiel 36:25; Hebrews 10:22).
  Held by most churches that baptise infants.

### The Lord's Supper (Holy Communion)

**What all agree on:** Jesus instituted the Supper on the night He was betrayed and told His followers to
"do this in remembrance of Me" (Luke 22:19-20; 1 Corinthians 11:23-26). It proclaims His death until He comes.

**What happens in the Supper?**
- **Memorial view** — the bread and cup are signs that help believers remember Christ's sacrifice and
  renew their trust in Him. Associated with Zwingli; common in Baptist and many independent churches.
- **Spiritual presence view** — Christ is truly present by His Spirit, and believers are spiritually
  nourished by Him as they receive in faith. Associated with Calvin; common in Reformed, Presbyterian and
  many Anglican churches.
- **Presence "in, with and under" the bread and wine** — Christ's body and blood are truly present with the
  elements. The Lutheran view.
- **Transubstantiation** — the bread and wine become Christ's body and blood while keeping their appearance.
  The Roman Catholic view (Orthodox churches hold a similar real change without that term).

### Spiritual gifts (including speaking in tongues and prophecy)

**What all agree on:** The Holy Spirit gives gifts to every believer for building up the church
(1 Corinthians 12:4-11; Romans 12:6-8), and love is the greater way (1 Corinthians 13).

**Do the "sign" gifts — tongues, prophecy, healing, miracles — continue today?**
- **Continuationism** — all the gifts continue until Christ returns (1 Corinthians 13:8-12; 1 Corinthians
  14:1, 39). Held by Pentecostal and charismatic churches, and by many in other churches.
- **Cessationism** — the miraculous sign gifts were given to confirm the apostles' message and laid the
  church's foundation, and ceased once the New Testament was complete (Ephesians 2:20; Hebrews 2:3-4). God
  still heals and works in answer to prayer.
- **Open but cautious** — the gifts may continue, but claims should be tested carefully against Scripture
  and practised in order (1 Corinthians 14:26-40; 1 Thessalonians 5:19-21).

### Church government

**What all agree on:** Christ is the head of the church (Ephesians 1:22; Colossians 1:18), and the church
needs faithful, qualified leaders (1 Timothy 3; Titus 1).

- **Episcopal** — oversight by bishops over many congregations, with priests or ministers and deacons
  (Anglican, Methodist, Catholic, Orthodox; in Kenya also many Pentecostal and independent churches led by
  bishops).
- **Presbyterian** — rule by elders, together in the local church and in regional and national assemblies
  (Presbyterian and Reformed churches; Acts 15; Titus 1:5).
- **Congregational** — each local church governs itself, with decisions made by its members and its
  leaders (Baptist, many independent and Pentecostal churches; Matthew 18:17; Acts 6:3).

### Election and free will (predestination)

**What all agree on:** Salvation is God's work, received by grace through faith (Ephesians 2:8-9; Article 11(F)),
and the gospel is to be preached to all people.

- **Reformed (Calvinist) view** — before creation God chose, out of His grace alone, those He would save, and
  He brings them to faith (Ephesians 1:4-5; Romans 8:29-30; John 6:44).
- **Arminian / Wesleyan view** — God desires all to be saved and gives grace that enables people to respond;
  election is based on God's foreknowledge of those who will believe (1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9; John 3:16).
- Some traditions hold positions between these — for example, Lutherans teach that God elects to salvation by
  grace alone, while those who are lost are lost through their own unbelief, not by God's choice.

### Can a true believer lose their salvation?

**What all agree on:** Salvation is by God's grace, and believers are called to persevere in faith and holiness.

- **Perseverance of the saints** — those truly born again are kept by God and will go on in faith and holiness
  to the end; someone who finally falls away shows they were never truly born again (John 10:28-29;
  Philippians 1:6; Romans 8:38-39; 1 John 2:19). The Reformed view.
- **Eternal security ("once saved, always saved")** — a person who has truly believed can never lose their
  salvation. Held by many Baptist and evangelical Christians, including many who are not Reformed on election.
  (Reformed teachers often prefer "perseverance", stressing that true believers keep living by faith.)
- **Salvation can be lost** — a believer can turn away from Christ and be lost (Hebrews 6:4-6; Galatians 5:4;
  2 Peter 2:20-21). Held by many Wesleyan, Methodist and Pentecostal Christians, and in Lutheran, Catholic and
  Orthodox teaching.

### The end times

**What all agree on:** Jesus Christ will return personally and visibly, the dead will be raised, and God will
judge the world in righteousness (Article 11(I); Acts 1:11; 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17).

**The "thousand years" of Revelation 20:**
- **Premillennialism** — Christ returns *before* a literal thousand-year reign on earth. *Dispensational*
  premillennialists usually expect believers to be "raptured" before a period of great tribulation
  (pre-tribulation); *historic* premillennialists expect the church to go through the tribulation, with
  Christ gathering His people at His return.
- **Amillennialism** — the thousand years is symbolic of Christ's present reign, from His resurrection until
  His return.
- **Postmillennialism** — the gospel will spread and transform the world in a long age of blessing, after
  which Christ returns.

### Women in church leadership

**What all agree on:** Men and women are equally made in God's image (Genesis 1:27), equally saved in Christ
(Galatians 3:28), and equally gifted by the Spirit to serve.

- **Complementarian view** — men and women are equal in worth with some differing roles; the office of
  elder/pastor and the authoritative teaching of the gathered church are for qualified men
  (1 Timothy 2:12; 1 Timothy 3:1-7).
- **Egalitarian view** — all ministries and leadership roles are open to both men and women according to
  their gifting (Galatians 3:28; Romans 16:1-7; Acts 2:17-18).

In Kenya, practice differs between churches: the Anglican Church of Kenya, for example, has ordained women as
priests since the 1980s and as bishops since 2021, while other churches reserve the office of pastor or bishop
for men.

### The days of creation

**What all agree on:** God created all things out of nothing by His word (Genesis 1:1; Hebrews 11:3; Article 11(B)).

- **Young-earth view** — God created in six ordinary days, a few thousand years ago.
- **Old-earth views** — the earth is very old, and the "days" are read as long ages (day-age), or as a
  literary framework presenting God's ordered work rather than a timetable (framework), or with a long gap
  before Genesis 1:2 (gap view).
- **Evolutionary creation** — God created living things through processes He designed and sustains, including
  evolution, while humanity is uniquely made in His image. Held by some evangelicals; many others reject it.

### The day of worship (Sabbath or Sunday)

**What all agree on:** God gave rest and worship as gifts, and Christians gather to worship Him (Hebrews 10:25).

- **Sunday, the Lord's Day** — Christians worship on the first day of the week, the day of Christ's
  resurrection (Acts 20:7; 1 Corinthians 16:2; Revelation 1:10). Held by most churches; some (for example in
  the Presbyterian tradition) keep Sunday as the "Christian Sabbath", a whole day of rest and worship.
- **Saturday Sabbath** — the seventh-day Sabbath of the commandments remains the day of rest and worship
  (Exodus 20:8-11). Held by Seventh-day Adventists and some other churches.
- Some also teach that in Christ no day is required above another (Romans 14:5-6; Colossians 2:16-17).

## Christian living questions

These are questions about how to live, rather than doctrines. The Bible gives clear principles, but it does
not always name the modern situation, and churches apply the principles in different ways. For each, the
companion explains what the Scriptures teach and how churches differ. Where DeKUTCU's own Constitution or
Code of Conduct sets a standard for its members, that standard is stated as **DeKUTCU's own standard**;
beyond those, DeKUTCU does not lay down a rule.

### Betting and gambling

**What the Bible teaches (principles all agree on):** The Bible does not mention betting by name, but warns
against the love of money and the desire to get rich quickly (1 Timothy 6:9-10; Proverbs 13:11; Proverbs
28:20), calls believers to contentment (Hebrews 13:5), to work for what they earn (2 Thessalonians 3:10-12;
Ephesians 4:28) and to be faithful stewards of what God gives (Luke 16:10-11), and teaches that we should not
be mastered by anything (1 Corinthians 6:12).

**How churches differ:**
- **Betting is wrong** — most churches in Kenya (evangelical, Pentecostal, AIC and many mainline churches)
  teach that Christians should not bet at all: it feeds the love of money, depends on others' losses, wastes
  what God has entrusted, and often becomes an addiction.
- **Small stakes may be allowed** — some traditions, including Roman Catholic teaching, hold that games of
  chance and wagers are not wrong in themselves, but become wrong when they take what a person needs for
  themselves or others, and warn that a passion for gambling can become a kind of slavery (Catechism of the
  Catholic Church, 2413).

**DeKUTCU's own standard:** the Code of Conduct does not address betting specifically.

**If betting has become a struggle:** debt and addiction from betting are common among students, and there is
no shame in asking for help. A leader can walk with you and help you find support.

### Tithing and giving

**What all agree on:** Christians are called to give generously and cheerfully to God's work and to people in
need (2 Corinthians 9:6-7; Acts 20:35), and giving is an act of worship, not a way to earn God's favour.

- **The tithe (10%) is the standard** — the tithe predates the Law (Genesis 14:20; Genesis 28:22), Malachi
  3:10 calls God's people to bring it, and Jesus affirmed it (Matthew 23:23). Many churches teach a tenth of
  income as the starting point for giving.
- **Generous, proportionate giving** — the tithe belonged to the Old Testament Law; the New Testament teaches
  giving "as each one has decided in his heart" and in proportion to income (2 Corinthians 9:7;
  1 Corinthians 16:2), for many people more than 10%.

DeKUTCU's own work is funded by members' tithes, offerings and thanksgiving (Constitution, Article 57).

### Dress and appearance

**What all agree on:** Believers should dress modestly and with self-control, and God looks at the heart rather
than outward appearance (1 Timothy 2:9-10; 1 Peter 3:3-4; 1 Samuel 16:7).

- **Specific dress rules** — some churches apply these principles with particular rules, for example about
  trousers for women, head coverings, hair, jewellery or make-up (drawing on passages such as
  Deuteronomy 22:5 and 1 Corinthians 11:2-16).
- **Principles applied by conscience** — other churches teach the principles of modesty and humility and leave
  the particular choices to the believer's conscience and the culture they live in.

**DeKUTCU's own standard:** the dress code applies to members *ministering* - not to every member attending (as
DeKUTCU applies it, confirmed 2026-10-15). Members ministering at CU meetings (leading worship, ushering, moderating, praying
publicly, giving announcements) follow the dress code in the Code of Conduct (Articles 8-10), given "not as
legalism, but decency, modesty and freedom from distraction in worship", in a spirit of love and without
shaming. For ladies: a modest cover-up over body-conforming outfits and official trousers; chest, bust and
sleeveless garments covered; skirts and dresses knee-length or longer, without inappropriate slits; nothing
excessively tight; no shorts. For gentlemen: no body-conforming or sagged trousers; chest covered; no shorts;
a well-groomed appearance. For both: closed or smart open shoes - no crocs, sandals or slides while
ministering.

### Dating and marriage across denominations

**What all agree on:** Believers should marry believers (2 Corinthians 6:14; 1 Corinthians 7:39), marriage is a
lifelong covenant between a man and a woman (Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:4-6), and sexual purity is God's will
(1 Thessalonians 4:3-5).

**Different denominations:** marrying a fellow believer from another denomination is not forbidden by Scripture,
and many churches welcome it. Couples are wise to talk honestly, early, about where they will worship, how
their children will be baptised and brought up, and what each church expects (for example in weddings or
dowry customs), and to seek counsel from their families and church leaders.

**DeKUTCU's own standard:** the Code of Conduct holds that sexual intimacy is reserved for marriage between a man
and a woman, and calls members to purity, self-control and holiness in their relationships (Article 11).
Cohabitation and sexual immorality are not permitted among members; such a matter is first met with gracious
counsel, and only then, if needed, suspension from leadership or ministry, with follow-up and a way back
(Articles 12 and 15). Where a member becomes pregnant or is responsible for a pregnancy, the response is
pastoral and confidential, not punitive, and no one is shamed or excluded from fellowship (Article 13).

### Alcohol

**What all agree on:** Drunkenness is sin (Ephesians 5:18; Galatians 5:21), and believers must not cause
others to stumble (Romans 14:21).

- **Total abstinence** — it is wise and loving to avoid alcohol altogether, especially given its harm in
  society. Taught by many African churches.
- **Moderation** — drinking in moderation is permitted (Psalm 104:15; John 2:1-11), while drunkenness is sin.

**DeKUTCU's own standard:** the Code of Conduct treats substance abuse - including alcoholism and drug abuse - as
a conduct matter: a member involved is suspended from ministry for at least two months, with consistent
follow-up, and may be restored on repentance (Articles 14 and 15). It does not rule on drinking in moderation.

### Secular music and entertainment

**What all agree on:** Whatever believers do should honour God (1 Corinthians 10:31), and they are to fill their
minds with what is true, pure and lovely (Philippians 4:8).

- **Avoid secular music and entertainment** — some churches teach that believers should keep to Christian music
  and avoid secular entertainment, so as not to be shaped by the world (Romans 12:2; 1 John 2:15-16).
- **Enjoy what is wholesome** — other churches teach that music, sport and entertainment are part of God's good
  creation, to be enjoyed with discernment, avoiding what is ungodly in content or setting (1 Timothy 4:4-5).

**DeKUTCU's own standard:** the Code of Conduct says that a member taking part in secular activities in a way that
compromises their Christian witness (for example secular singing or dancing of that kind) is suspended from
ministry for at least two months, with follow-up and a way back (Articles 14 and 15).

## How the companion uses this guide

When a member asks about a **secondary issue**, the companion:

1. explains what the relevant Bible passages say, without drawing a conclusion on the disputed point;
2. describes the main views fairly, using this guide;
3. says clearly that DeKUTCU does not take a position, and encourages the member to study the Scriptures and
   talk with their own church or a leader.

For a **Christian living question**, it explains the Bible's principles and how churches apply them, states
DeKUTCU's own standard where the Constitution or Code of Conduct sets one, and where someone is struggling
(for example with betting, debt or substance abuse) it offers to connect them with a leader.

On the **fundamental truths** (Article 11), the companion speaks plainly, because DeKUTCU holds them.
