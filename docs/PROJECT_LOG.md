## Stage detail

### Stage 0 — Environment & Access Setup
Python, VS Code, Git/GitHub, Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, Groq API, all working. Free-tier-only stack throughout (no budget) — see the tech-stack decision entries below.

### Stage 1 — Skeleton WhatsApp bot
Flask webhook live on Render (`dekutcu-engagement-system.onrender.com`), full WhatsApp round-trip confirmed for real. Fixed along the way: Render free-tier sleep (UptimeRobot ping every 5 min), a Meta WABA-not-subscribed-to-app bug, and 24h access-token expiry (permanently fixed via a System User permanent token).

### Stage 2 — Data Layer
`members` table. `reg_number` (unique, NOT NULL) is the true permanent identity — school-assigned, survives a phone number change. `whatsapp_id` is a nullable pointer, re-linked when a member messages from a new number. Migrated from a flat script to an app-factory Flask package, and from SQLite to PostgreSQL/Neon (Render's own Postgres free tier expires after 30 days with no backups — incompatible with the project timeline).

### Stage 3 — Registration & Consent
Full WhatsApp conversation: reg number (normalized) → name → gender → year of study → area (10 fixed areas) → **two separate consents** (data/tracking/placement, and proactive follow-up check-ins — opting out of follow-up does NOT affect active-membership/welfare status). Field-name correction at the review step; `cancel` = full restart anywhere. Returning members auto-relink by `reg_number` from a new phone.

### Stage 4 — Intent Router
STOP/RESUME keywords checked globally, hardcoded, before registration status and before the LLM — an opt-out must never depend on an AI classifier having a good day. Everything else routes through Groq-based classification. Genuinely implemented: `update_details` (area only, see Stage 5 detail), `unsubscribe_followup`, `resume_followup`, `withdraw_data_consent`, plus everything added during Stage 5 (see below). Deliberately still stubbed, pending their own stage: `general_question`, `checkin_response`, `feedback_response`, `purchase_study_guide`, `request_human`, `needs_support`. (`event_rsvp` genuinely implemented as of Stage 6, see below.)

### Stage 5 — Allocation Engine (Objective 2)
Core engine (`app/services/allocation.py`): three interchangeable functions sharing the same sizing logic — `allocate_members_greedy` (fast round-robin baseline), `allocate_members_ilp` (PuLP/CBC, exact optimization), `allocate_members_topup` (only places *new* members, leaves existing placements alone — see decision log). Wired into WhatsApp as `allocate_groups`/`reshuffle_groups`, background-threaded (the ILP is too slow for a synchronous webhook reply), with a real leader confirmation flow. **Verified live end-to-end** against 200 real+synthetic members on the deployed Render app, not just locally.

While finishing this stage's real-world WhatsApp deployment, three pieces of infrastructure turned out to be genuine prerequisites and got built alongside it, even though they aren't named stages in the original 15-stage plan:
- **Group leader management** — recruiting and tracking who leads each area's Bible study group(s), distinct from `is_leader` (which gates exec/org actions). Needed before "your leader is X" could mean anything.
- **Area-change reassignment** — a member changing residence area needs their group membership handled without a leader losing track of it.
- **`group_query` tool-calling** — after the leader-management and reassignment work produced a growing pile of narrow intents (view leaders, resolve pending, etc.), read-only *questions* about groups/leaders/membership moved to LLM tool-calling instead of one hand-coded intent per question shape. See decision log for why, and why actions stayed on the fixed-intent path.

### Stage 6 — Event & RSVP Manager
Designed directly from the proposal's own scope section (1.6) rather than assumption -- it specifies a real fork this stage had to build around: **"tracked" events** (Bible Study, cell group, fellowship) get individual RSVP, organization-wide to every data-consenting member with a group placement (confirmed directly, not scoped to one group/area); **"broadcast" events** (Sunday service) are announcement-only, explicitly "individual follow-up expectations do not apply". `events` (`event_type`, title, a real `DATE` column so "what's coming up" can actually filter past events in SQL, time/location/description as plain text, `created_by`) and `event_rsvps` (one row per event+member, `ON CONFLICT` upsert so changing your mind overwrites rather than duplicates) — two new tables, `app/models/event.py` / `event_rsvp.py`.

Two new pending-state tables (`pending_event_creation`, `pending_rsvps`) mirror the shape every other guided flow in this project already uses. Three intents: `create_event` (new, leader-only, a 6-step guided flow — type → title → date → time → location → description → confirm, broadcasting on confirm) and a split of the previously-conflated `event_rsvp` stub into `list_events` (read-only, a plain direct answer rather than LLM tool-calling, since "what's coming up" is one fixed shape unlike `group_query`'s genuinely varied phrasings) and `event_rsvp` (the actual RSVP action, numbered-list disambiguation if more than one tracked event is open — same "never free-text parse a selection" rule used everywhere else in this project).

**Deliberately not built in this stage**, per the proposal's own component split: attendance confirmation (Stage 7's job — reason capture needs to know someone didn't show before asking why, so `event_rsvps` is intentionally left extendable rather than pre-building a column Stage 7 hasn't earned yet), the bandit (Stage 8), leader-side "who's coming" reporting (Stage 13 — the RSVP data is already sitting there for it), and post-event feedback collection (raised as an open question, left undecided rather than assumed either way).

**Verified end-to-end against the real database and the real webhook entry point** (`route_incoming_message`, not a shortcut around it), WhatsApp sends mocked: full leader creation flow for both event types, invalid-date rejection then correction, broadcast correctly reaching only the one real-`whatsapp_id` member among 200 (mostly-synthetic) test members, the tracked-vs-broadcast notification wording difference (RSVP invite vs plain announcement), multi-event RSVP disambiguation via numbered list, and `cancel` working mid-flow in both the creation and RSVP conversations.

### Stage 7 — Reason Capture & Classifier (Bible Study slice)
Scoped in two deliberately separate slices per the Objective 3 scope-evolution decision above -- this entry covers only the Bible Study side; the open-fellowship check-in/regular-attendee-inference side is separate, not-yet-built machinery that will eventually write to the same `absences` table.

**The first genuinely proactive trigger in this project.** Every other stage only ever runs in response to an incoming WhatsApp message; this stage needs to wake itself up at a specific time (Tuesday 9pm Nairobi time) with nothing having been sent to it first. New generic mechanism, `app/services/scheduler.py`: one more background thread (same pattern as the message-queue workers), checking every 5 minutes whether any registered `(name, weekday, hour, func)` task is due, guarded against firing twice in the same hour-wide window by a new `scheduled_task_runs` table. Built generically so a second scheduled task (the fellowship check-ins) can be added later without a rewrite. Required adding `tzdata` as an explicit dependency -- `zoneinfo` needs it for the IANA database on Windows (Linux, which Render actually runs, usually has it system-wide, but relying on that isn't portable, and it's what actually broke local testing here).

**The flow**: Tuesday 9pm → every leader currently matched to a specific Bible Study group (`leads_group_label`) gets their group's roster and is asked who was absent, comma-separated numbers or 'none' (new `pending_attendance_marking` table). For each person marked absent, an `absences` row is created and -- if they have a WhatsApp number on file -- they're asked directly why they missed (new `pending_reason_capture` table, pointing at the specific `absences` row by ID, not by re-deriving it from date/type, to avoid any lookup ambiguity). Their free-text reply is classified by the LLM into the proposal's six categories, plus a `shows_distress` flag from the same classification call.

**Real bug found and fixed during testing**: `absences` was originally keyed by `whatsapp_id NOT NULL`, which crashed the moment a leader marked absent a member with no WhatsApp number on file (most of the 200 test members, by design -- only one has a real number). The fix was switching the key to `reg_number` -- this project's own permanent-identity principle since Stage 2, which got overlooked writing this table specifically. An absence is real, recordable data even for a member we can't currently reach to ask why; the *message* to them is what's conditionally skipped, not the record itself. Caught by a real end-to-end test with synthetic (no-WhatsApp-number) members, not by an isolated unit test that would have supplied a whatsapp_id by construction.

**Also fixed during testing, smaller**: the leader's confirmation after marking absences originally claimed "reached out to them directly" unconditionally, even for members who have no WhatsApp number and were never actually messaged. Now honestly splits the confirmation into who was actually reached vs. who couldn't be.

**The distress safety-net, deliberately just a flag, not an escalation** (Stage 11 doesn't exist yet): if `shows_distress` comes back true, the member gets a reply pointing them toward a leader directly, instead of the same generic "thanks for sharing" everyone else gets. Verified live with a real classification call: a routine reason ("I had a CAT that day") correctly classified as `scheduling_conflict` / distress=false; a reply reading "I've been feeling really hopeless and overwhelmed" correctly classified as `personal_difficulty` / distress=true and triggered the different reply.

**Verified end-to-end**, real database, real webhook entry point, real Groq classification calls (not mocked): the scheduler fires correctly on a mocked Tuesday-9pm Nairobi timestamp and not outside that window, doesn't double-fire within the same day: the nudge → leader marks absentees → absence records created with correct `reg_number` → reason-capture sent only to reachable members → free-text reply classified correctly for both a routine and a distress-signaling case → 'skip' handled correctly.

**Not yet built**: the open-fellowship check-in/regular-attendee-inference slice (needs the fellowship check-in timing decided first), and anything past recording -- Stage 8 (the bandit itself) hasn't started.

## Design decisions log

Newest entries at the bottom. Each entry: what was decided, and why — not the back-and-forth that got there.

**Tech stack is free-tier only, throughout.** No budget for this project. Groq chosen over Gemini as the primary classification LLM specifically because intent routing fires on *every* incoming message and needs Groq's higher free daily request volume; Gemini was originally kept available for later, lower-frequency, quality-sensitive work (e.g. the future RAG companion) -- **superseded 2026-09-26, see the design decisions log near the bottom of this file: RAG will use DeepSeek instead, Gemini turned out to require billing too.** Neon chosen over Render's own Postgres (which expires after 30 days on the free tier with no backups) and over Supabase/Koyeb, for auto-wake-on-query behavior and better solo-troubleshooting docs.

**`reg_number`, not `whatsapp_id`, is the permanent member identity.** Phone numbers change; school registration numbers don't. `whatsapp_id` is just a nullable pointer that gets re-linked.

**Consent is two separate flags, not one.** `data_consent` (storing details, placement, welfare tracking) and `followup_consent` (proactive check-ins) are independent — opting out of check-ins must never be interpreted as opting out of active membership.

**STOP/RESUME are hardcoded keywords, checked before anything else, including registration status.** An opt-out mechanism can't depend on an LLM classifier working correctly that day.

**Masters students and staff are out of scope for automated allocation.** Rare in practice; handled manually. The system currently only validates `year_of_study` 1–6 and has no separate staff/masters concept at all.

**10 fixed areas, hard-partitioned before optimization.** Exact string match at registration (closest-match prompt if unclear), no GPS/adjacency logic. The allocation engine treats areas as completely independent — every algorithm runs per-area, never across areas.

**Allocation group size is 8–12, not the original 6–10 spec.** Changed during direct design work on the algorithm itself (supersedes the number in any earlier planning notes). Group count per area is chosen to minimize the *worst* individual group's deviation from [8,12] (0 if a perfect fit exists), tie-broken toward more, more-evenly-sized groups — this specifically closes a gap where a strict "must fit exactly" rule leaves areas of 13/14/15 members with no valid group count at all (verified: without this, a real 200-member run mis-flagged 3 normal-sized areas as "too small").

**ILP minimizes the single worst gender/year deviation, gender and year checked separately — not jointly.** With only 8–12 people per group, joint (gender × year) cells are too sparse to usefully optimize, and the original balance objective was stated as two separate proportions anyway. Minimizing the *worst* case (minimax) rather than the *average* directly targets avoiding one badly-skewed group, which is what motivated building the ILP over the greedy version in the first place (a real greedy run produced a 6-male/3-female group in an area that was exactly 50/50 overall).

**ILP solve is capped at 15 seconds per area.** Finding a very good (often exactly optimal) answer is fast; what can run unboundedly long is the solver *proving* nothing better exists, by ruling out symmetric group-label duplicates it has no way to know are interchangeable. Uncapped, a ~45-person/5-group area was observed to hang for minutes at 200-member scale. Capped, quality is unaffected in testing.

**Top-up allocation never moves an already-placed member.** Allocation reruns periodically as members trickle in; a full reshuffle every time would break group continuity/relationships for no reason. Only genuinely new (unplaced) members get processed; leftover new members who don't fit any existing group form fresh groups of their own. `reshuffle_groups` (full, from-scratch regeneration) exists as a separate, more seriously-confirmed action for when a real reset is actually wanted.

**Group leaders are a different concept from `is_leader`.** `is_leader` gates exec/organizational actions (running allocation, reports). A group leader runs one specific area's Bible study and is a completely separate role — tracked via `leader_of_area` (confirmed) and `leads_group_label` (matched to a *specific* formed group, once allocation has actually formed enough groups in their area). Leaders are recruited **before** allocation runs (mirroring the real off-platform process: an exec leader asks around informally, then formalizes it on-platform), since they're naturally tied to an area, not a numbered group that doesn't exist yet.

**Every leader-facing "pick a person/group" flow uses a numbered list, never free-text name parsing.** Matching a name from free text requires disambiguation infrastructure (fuzzy matching, "which John did you mean") that doesn't exist and wasn't worth building for this. Applies to nominating a leader, resolving a pending nomination, removing a leader, and resolving a member's area-change reassignment — all numbered-list selection from a bounded candidate set instead.

**A member's area change never silently moves them out of their existing group.** Their `group_label` is left untouched so routine top-up runs ignore them; instead a recommendation is computed (reusing the exact same heuristic top-up uses for new members) and a leader has to act on it via the same numbered-list pattern, approving the recommendation or overriding it. All three affected parties get notified: the member (once resolved, naming their new group and leader), the leader who resolves it, and the *old* group's leader (their roster just shrank).

**Read-only questions about groups/leaders/membership use LLM tool-calling instead of one hand-coded intent per question shape; actions that change data stay on fixed intents with confirmation.** Adding a new intent for every phrasing of every possible question doesn't scale, and was already showing strain (a growing pile of narrow "view X" intents). The distinction that matters: getting a tool choice wrong for a *question* just produces an unhelpful answer; getting an *action* wrong changes something for someone without proper confirmation. So allocation, leader nomination, and reassignment resolution all remain fixed, confirmed intents — `group_query` handles open-ended questions by giving the LLM a small toolbox (own group; own led group if a group leader; org-wide listing/roster tools if an exec leader) scoped dynamically to the asker's actual role, never trusted from the LLM's own request. Verified the underlying Groq model supports tool-calling before building around it, and verified permission scoping empirically (a regular member's org-wide question is correctly declined because the tool is never offered to them, not just prompted against).

**`group_query` bugs found via a real live phone test, all fixed (2026-09-24).** A genuinely useful stress test — five distinct real bugs surfaced from actual use that hadn't shown up in earlier scripted testing: (1) area name matching was exact-string, case-sensitive, and the model wasn't reliably preserving the user's casing when building tool calls, so "bomas" (lowercase) sometimes returned zero groups; fixed by making the database match case-insensitively and by substring. (2) No partial/alias matching at all — "internal" for "Internal Hostels" failed outright; fixed by telling the model the canonical area list up front and having it resolve loose names before calling a tool. (3) Looking up one specific group required knowing the exact "Area #N" label, which no user has reason to know; added a loose-identifier resolver that falls back to "this area's only group" when unambiguous. (4) The model conflated "who leads the group I belong to" with "am I a leader" — confirmed live, a real group leader was told "No, you're not a leader." Fixed by renaming the ambiguous field and being explicit in both the tool descriptions and system prompt about the distinction. (5) Some phrasings intermittently landed on the older, less-capable `view_groups` intent instead of `group_query`, silently losing detail (a request for full lists "with members" came back as counts-only). Retired `view_groups` entirely rather than trying to out-word the ambiguity — its behavior is fully subsumed by `group_query`'s `list_groups` tool, so nothing was lost. All five re-verified against real data after fixing.

**Deferred, explicitly agreed to tackle separately: the whole bot has no conversation memory, at any level.** Every message — both the top-level intent classifier and each `group_query` call — is handled as if it's the first thing ever said, with zero memory of the immediately preceding turn. This isn't a `group_query`-specific bug; it's a pre-existing property of the whole architecture that `group_query` just made far more *visible*, because its open-ended phrasing invites natural follow-ups ("then what am I", a bare corrected spelling after a failed lookup) in a way the old rigid fixed-intent flows didn't. Deliberately not patched in a hurry — deciding how much history to carry, for how long, and at what per-message token cost needs its own proper design pass, not a bolt-on.

**Conversation memory — DONE (2026-09-24, pushed as `28071e6`), `app/models/conversation_history.py`.** Follow-up to the deferred item above, designed properly rather than bolted on. A bounded rolling log (last 10 messages, both sides) per `whatsapp_id`, wired into two places: `classify_intent` (so short/ambiguous messages classify correctly using recent context) and `group_query`'s tool-calling loop (same, for interpreting a follow-up in context). Deliberately scoped to only the general, freely-classified chat path — the other conversational flows (registration, pending-action confirmations, leader nomination, area change) already have their own dedicated step-tracking and don't go through ambiguous LLM classification mid-flow, so they don't need this.
  - **Window size: 10 messages (~5 exchanges).** Enough for a short clarification chain without letting every classification call grow unboundedly as a conversation gets longer; Groq's fast/free-tier-generous enough that this is a cheap default either way.
  - **Consent**: this is the first table in the project to store raw message text rather than structured/extracted fields, worth naming explicitly given how central informed consent is to this project's design. Decided it's covered under the existing `data_consent` — it's operational data in service of the same already-consented conversation, not a new purpose, never leader-visible — rather than needing its own consent prompt. But `withdraw_data_consent` now also purges it, so withdrawing genuinely means no trace is kept.
  - **Logging order matters**: the current exchange is logged AFTER the handler produces a reply, not before -- otherwise a call's own history fetch would double up with the message it's currently processing.
  - Verified live: "and number 3?" (meaningless without context) correctly resolved to "Bomas #3" right after asking about "Bomas number 2", using only the logged history; consent withdrawal correctly purged a test history to zero rows.

**Groq's free-tier daily token cap (200,000 TPD) is a real operational constraint, confirmed by hitting it during round-2 testing (2026-09-24).** A single day of heavy manual testing (mine and Keziah's combined, ~220 requests) burned ~270K tokens against the 200K/day limit — confirmed via Groq's Organization Usage dashboard, which showed near-zero usage on every prior day and a single sharp spike on the test day. Once exceeded, `classify_intent` and `answer_group_question` both silently fall back (by design — see their docstrings) to `"unclear"`/an apology rather than erroring loudly, which made a genuine quota exhaustion look identical to a logic bug for a while during debugging (traced via Groq's raw HTTP response headers and the exception log line, not obvious from the bot's replies alone). `group_query`'s tool-calling loop is the main cost driver — full system prompt + tool schemas + conversation history + up to `_MAX_TOOL_ROUNDS` round trips, per question. **Implication for the live presentation**: a burst of rapid audience questions could exhaust the same daily budget mid-demo if there's been other testing that day. No code change made for this (out of scope for a free-tier project) — noting it as a known risk to manage operationally: avoid heavy testing the day of/right before presenting, and check Groq's usage dashboard beforehand rather than assuming headroom.

**`group_query` round 2 — multi-group-area handling, all fixed (2026-09-24).** A follow-up live test (this time run from a written 14-question script covering permission tiers, area matching, loose lookup, full-listing formatting, and conversation memory) surfaced five more real bugs, all one underlying pattern: no consistent handling for a request that spans *every* group in an area rather than one specific group. (1) "What about internal hostels" (a follow-up after a different area's question succeeded) fell to a generic apology instead of resolving to that area. (2) "I want the full details of the groups in bomas" — same generic fallback. (3) "Names of the members of all groups in bomas" — no tool existed for "every roster in an area in one call", so the model had nothing reliable to chain. (4) Replying "All of them" to the bot's own "which group did you mean?" clarifying question wasn't recognized as answering that question. (5) "What of the members" (a follow-up after a groups-summary list) also fell to the generic fallback. Fixed by: adding a new `get_area_rosters` tool (every group in an area, each with its full roster, in one call — instead of the model having to improvise chaining several single-group lookups); substantially expanding `_SYSTEM_PROMPT` with explicit decision rules for area-wide requests (summary → `list_groups`, names → `get_area_rosters`, "which one?" replied with "all of them" → resolve to whichever the original ask wanted); raising `_MAX_TOOL_ROUNDS` 3→4 (multi-group answers sometimes need an extra round); replacing the bare apology with a more actionable `_FALLBACK_MESSAGE` that invites a narrower retry. All five re-verified against real data after fixing.

**Found and fixed alongside round 2: `classify_intent` could misroute an in-topic follow-up to `general_question`, bypassing `group_query` entirely.** While re-testing bug (1) above, "What about internal hostels" started returning the *unrelated* `general_question` stub text — a different, worse failure than the original bug, since the message never even reached `group_query`'s tools/prompt fixes. Root cause: `general_question`'s definition was generic enough to compete with `group_query` for any message that reads fine as a standalone topic (hostel facilities, in this case) even when the immediately preceding exchange was clearly about Bible study groups. Adding a generic "use history to resolve short/vague follow-ups" instruction wasn't enough — the model still needs a concrete example naming this exact ambiguity to reliably override it. Fixed by narrowing `general_question`'s definition to explicitly exclude group topics, and adding a worked example to the classifier's system prompt (`_build_system_prompt` in `intent_router.py`) showing precisely this "named a plausible standalone topic, but history says it's actually a group follow-up" case. Also surfaced a testing-hygiene issue along the way: the shared manual-test `whatsapp_id`'s conversation history was carrying stale rows across unrelated test rounds from earlier sessions, which was polluting classification context for new tests — worth clearing (`clear_conversation_history`) before each fresh manual test run, not just relying on the 10-message window to age it out.

**Groq rate-limit resilience — two changes, both done (2026-09-24): short retry-backoff, and async webhook processing.** The round-2 testing above pushed real usage past Groq's free-tier limits, surfacing an architectural gap: a `RateLimitError` had no retry at all, and `classify_intent`/`group_query` both silently fell back to their generic "unclear"/apology replies on ANY API error, making a brief rate-limit blip look identical to a real logic bug (this cost significant debugging time before the actual cause -- quota exhaustion, confirmed via Groq's Organization Usage dashboard showing a single-day spike to ~270K tokens against the 200K/day cap -- was found).
  - **`app/services/groq_client.py`** (new): a shared `create_chat_completion()` wrapper used by both `classify_intent` and `answer_group_question`, retrying only `groq.RateLimitError` specifically (not other error types, which retrying can't fix) up to 2 times with short exponential backoff (0.5s, 1s). Kept deliberately short rather than honoring Groq's own suggested wait (which can be minutes) -- see next point for why.
  - **Async webhook processing** (`app/routes/webhook.py`, `app/__init__.py`): incoming messages are now pushed onto an in-process `queue.Queue` and handled by a single background worker thread, instead of being processed inline during the webhook POST. Previously, `receive_message` ran the full classify → route → reply → send chain *before* returning `200` to Meta -- exactly the pattern already known to be risky (the same reason allocation was backgrounded in Stage 5), since a slow reply risks Meta assuming delivery failed and resending the same message, causing duplicate processing. Now Meta gets acked in milliseconds regardless of how long Groq takes downstream.
    - Only safe with exactly one gunicorn worker process -- confirmed via Render's Start Command (`gunicorn run:app`, no `-w` flag, defaults to 1). With more than one process, each would have its own separate in-memory queue and Meta's messages could split across them unpredictably; would need a DB-backed queue instead if that ever changes.
    - Deliberately ONE global worker (strict FIFO across all senders), not one thread per message or per sender. A single serial worker trivially guarantees a sender's own messages are never processed out of order or racing each other against shared pending-state rows (registration, leader nomination, area-change, etc.) -- correctness by construction, not by careful locking. The cost is that all users share one processing queue, so a burst adds latency for whoever's at the back of it; judged an acceptable trade at this project's scale (a delayed WhatsApp reply is normal; a wrong-looking one isn't).
    - The worker's broad `except Exception` is load-bearing, not a code smell: with only one worker thread, an unhandled exception on ANY single message would kill the thread and silently freeze the bot for every message after it -- a failure mode that couldn't happen under the old one-thread-per-request model, where each request was independent.
    - Verified via 3 tests against the real Flask app (`test_client`, real DB, `send_whatsapp_message` mocked): webhook acks in ~2-4ms regardless of downstream processing time; two messages from the same sender fired back-to-back are proven to never overlap (second message's start time is always >= first message's end time, even with an artificial 1.5s delay injected into the first); a simulated crash on one message does not prevent the next message from being processed.
  - **What this does and doesn't solve**: together these fix the "bot looks broken under brief load" symptom -- retries absorb momentary per-minute collisions, and the queue means a slow/throttled reply is just late, never lost or wrong. Neither reduces total daily token consumption, which is the real constraint at real 200-member scale; that needs the separate token-cost-reduction work (shorter prompts, smaller history window, avoiding full-roster tool calls unless names were actually asked for) discussed but not yet implemented. A same-provider second key was ruled out (Groq's rate limit is org-level, and multiple free accounts to bypass it isn't something to build into an academically-defended project); Gemini (already in the stack, reserved for the future RAG stage) was identified as a plausible future fallback specifically for burst/per-minute relief, not yet implemented.

**Deliberately deferred, not forgotten -- scaling/resilience ideas discussed 2026-09-24 but not built, since none are urgent at current scale and all are better designed against real usage data than guesses:**
  - **DB-backed durable queue.** The current message queue (see above) lives only in memory -- if the app process restarts while messages are queued (a deploy, a crash), those specific messages are silently lost, unlike registration/leader-nomination state which is saved to Postgres and survives a restart fine. Fix: a `pending_messages` table, same pattern as the other `pending_*` tables, with the worker polling it instead of an in-memory `queue.Queue`. Zero-downside fix, just not yet built.
  - **Small sharded worker pool (2-3 lanes, keyed by sender) instead of one global worker.** Would meaningfully cut worst-case wait during a burst, while still guaranteeing any one sender's own messages stay in order (ordering only ever needed to be per-sender, not global). Capped at 2-3 deliberately -- Groq's own ~8,000 token/minute limit only fits ~6-7 typical messages regardless of how many worker threads exist, so a bigger pool wouldn't add real throughput without also adding another provider for the extra workers to call.
  - **Instant non-AI "got it, one moment!" filler reply when the queue is already backed up on arrival.** Addresses that WhatsApp silence reads as "broken" much faster than a website loading spinner would -- a person sees something immediately even if the real answer is still queued.
  - **Cerebras as a second OpenAI-compatible fallback provider** (alongside Groq), for `classify_intent` and `group_query`. Free tier ~1M tokens/day, same API shape as Groq (drop-in, unlike Gemini's differently-shaped function-calling), largest headroom-per-engineering-effort of the options considered.
  - **Gemini as a fallback specifically for `classify_intent`** (not `group_query` -- that needs Gemini's own, differently-shaped tool-calling implementation, real extra work, lower priority).
  - **Noted tension to revisit once Stage 12 (RAG) actually starts**: Gemini is earmarked as RAG's primary engine (large context, lower frequency) AND was floated as a fallback for Groq's overflow -- once RAG ships and has its own real load, those two jobs compete for the same Gemini quota. Cerebras (unclaimed by any other stage) is likely the more durable long-term fallback for the chat/classification path specifically because of this.
  - **Confirmed as non-issues for future stages**: the contextual bandit (Stage 8) runs as local math (LinUCB/Thompson Sampling), no API calls at all. Reward loop (Stage 10) is pure DB writes. M-Pesa (Stage 14) talks to Safaricom's API, not an LLM. Message Generator (Stage 9) is a real open design choice -- offline-pregenerated message variants per bandit arm (cheap, one-time cost) vs. live-generated-per-message (an ongoing per-interaction cost stacking on the same shared budget) -- to be decided when that stage starts, currently leaning toward pregenerated since bandit arms are normally a small, fixed set of choices anyway. Leadership Reporting (Stage 13) WILL add real ongoing load, similar in shape to `group_query`, since it's expected to reuse the same tool-calling pattern.

**Scaling/resilience round 2 -- durable queue, sharded workers, backlog filler, Cerebras fallback, and a real connection-pooling fix -- all DONE (2026-09-24).** Follow-up to the items deferred just above; built the same day rather than left open, once it was clear the fixes were cheap relative to the risk (RAG-specific tension noted above stays deferred -- everything here is scoped to the near-term chat/classification path only).
  - **`app/models/pending_message.py`** (new): the in-memory queue from the first round is gone, replaced with a `pending_messages` table -- same durability pattern as every other `pending_*` table. Workers claim rows via `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, filtered by `MOD(ABS(hashtext(sender_number)), num_shards) = shard_index` -- so each of the 3 background workers (`webhook.py`, `_NUM_WORKERS = 3`) only ever handles messages from its own assigned slice of senders. This is what guarantees any one sender's messages stay strictly in order (same sender always hashes to the same shard) while different senders process in parallel. Deliberately kept at 3, not maximized -- Groq's own per-minute limit only fits a handful of messages regardless of worker count, so a bigger pool doesn't add real throughput without another provider for the extra workers to call. A row is only deleted after it's actually been handled (success OR a caught failure), so a worker that crashed mid-processing can't silently lose a message.
  - **Backlog filler reply**: `enqueue_message()` returns how many other messages were already unclaimed at insert time (computed in the SAME connection as the insert -- see the pooling note below for why that matters); if that count is >= 3, `receive_message` sends an instant, non-AI "I'm a little busy, one moment" reply before the real one, since WhatsApp silence reads as "broken" much faster than a website's loading spinner would.
  - **`app/services/llm_client.py`** (renamed from `groq_client.py`, since it's no longer Groq-only): after Groq's own short retry (see round 1) is exhausted, falls back to Cerebras -- OpenAI-compatible, so it's the standard `openai` package pointed at `https://api.cerebras.ai/v1`, not a new SDK shape to learn. Callers (`classify_intent`, `group_query`) no longer know or care which provider actually answered -- they just pass `messages`/`tools`/etc. and get a completion back. Only triggers on `RateLimitError` specifically, from either provider -- a bad request or auth error wouldn't be fixed by switching providers, so those aren't retried or failed-over. Tool-call messages are now stored as plain dicts (`reply_message.model_dump()`) rather than the SDK's own message object, so a mid-conversation provider switch inside `group_query`'s tool-calling loop can't fail on trying to serialize a Groq-specific object type. `CEREBRAS_API_KEY` isn't in `.env` yet -- code degrades gracefully (skips straight to re-raising Groq's error) if it's absent, so this is safe to ship before signing up for Cerebras. Considered and set aside: Gemini as an additional/alternative fallback -- still valid per the discussion above, just not built yet, and there's real tension with Gemini's future dedicated RAG role worth resolving before leaning on it further.
  - **Real bug caught during testing, not just a nice-to-have: connection pooling (`app/database.py`).** Testing the durable queue's actual webhook latency surfaced that a single fresh Postgres connection to Neon cost multiple seconds in this environment -- meaning the "durable queue" fix had quietly reintroduced the exact kind of multi-second synchronous wait it was built to eliminate, just from Postgres instead of Groq. Root cause: `get_connection()` opened a brand-new physical connection on every single call, everywhere in the app, always -- invisible until now because every other synchronous path was already slow anyway (an LLM call). Fixed with `psycopg2.pool.ThreadedConnectionPool`, wrapped so `get_connection()`'s return value still responds to `.cursor()`/`.commit()`/`.close()` exactly as before (`.close()` now returns the connection to the pool instead of really closing it) -- meaning none of the ~10 other files already calling `get_connection()` needed to change at all. First attempt used `minconn=1`, which measured WORSE (8.4s) than no pooling at all (2.9s) -- the 3 background workers immediately started competing for the one pre-warmed connection in their poll loops, forcing the pool to keep creating new ones on demand DURING live operation instead of once at boot. Fixed by pre-warming `minconn=6` (enough for 3 workers + webhook headroom), which brought real webhook latency down to a stable ~1.1-1.2s -- the remaining time is normal network round-trip to Neon, not connection overhead, and is a fixed, bounded cost now, paid once at app startup for everything except that per-request round trip.
  - **Verified via automated tests against the real Flask app and real database** (not just unit-level mocks): a message enqueued with zero workers running survives untouched (restart-safety, directly); the backlog filler reply fires when backlogged and stays silent when not (isolated from real worker-timing races, which otherwise made the test flaky since these particular test messages have nothing slow to process, unlike a real Groq-bound message); all 3 round-1 properties (instant-ish ack, strict per-sender ordering, crash isolation) re-verified against the new sharded/durable design; 4 scenarios for the Cerebras fallback (Groq succeeds and Cerebras is never touched; Groq exhausts retries and Cerebras succeeds; Groq fails with no Cerebras key configured and the original error correctly propagates; both providers exhausted and the second failure correctly propagates).
  - **What's still explicitly not done**: Gemini as a further fallback (deferred, see above); the token-cost-reduction work (shorter prompts, smaller history window) that's the actual fix for sustained daily volume rather than burst resilience; verifying whether Render's real Render-to-Neon latency looks better than this local-machine-to-Neon measurement (plausible, not yet confirmed).

A real `models.list()` call against the account showed only two models actually available: `qwen-3.8-27b` and `gpt-oss-120b`. Picked `gpt-oss-120b` (same model family as the primary Groq model, `openai/gpt-oss-20b` -- prompts were tuned against this family's behavior specifically, so a same-family fallback is the safer bet). But a real `chat.completions.create()` call against EITHER model returned `402 Payment Required`, contradicting the "no credit card required" free tier described in earlier research -- likely the free tier now needs a billing step completed, or terms changed since. **Real regression found and fixed: the cross-provider-safety fix from round 2 had broken `group_query`'s normal Groq-only case (2026-09-26).** Storing `reply_message.model_dump()` (done specifically so a mid-loop provider switch wouldn't fail to serialize the SDK's own message object) turned out to include extra fields -- `annotations` specifically -- that Groq's own API doesn't recognize and rejects when they're echoed back on the next round of the SAME tool-calling loop. Every multi-round `group_query` answer was silently failing over to the generic apology, on Groq alone, with no other provider involved at all -- caught by a real end-to-end test (`answer_group_question` against a real member with a real group), not by the earlier mocked tests, which never exercised a genuine second round with real message content. Fixed by building a minimal dict by hand (`role`, `content`, and `tool_calls` only, each field re-built explicitly) instead of dumping the whole SDK object -- still provider-agnostic, but only carries the fields any OpenAI-compatible API actually expects back.

**Generic fallback mechanism verified end-to-end against a REAL provider, not just mocks (2026-09-26).** Mocked the primary Groq call to fail (deterministic, no quota spent), then let the fallback branch make an actual live call using a real key -- confirmed the whole chain works: detects the primary failure, reads the three env vars, builds a client, gets a genuine response back. **Major scope evolution for Objective 3 (the contextual bandit, Stages 7-8), settled 2026-09-26 -- broader than what the written proposal describes, recorded here explicitly so the evolution can be explained in the defense.** Prompted by two things happening at once: Keziah's own reservations about the bandit design, cross-checked against a detailed critique from a separate AI conversation (evaluated independently here, not accepted wholesale -- see below), and a real, concrete timeline problem: Stage 7/8 haven't started yet, Bible Study is weekly, and per the project's own Gantt chart November is reserved for evaluation/writing, not more data collection -- meaning a Bible-Study-only design would likely produce a single-digit number of real absence/follow-up/return cycles for the entire deployment, nowhere near enough to say anything defensible about whether the bandit learned anything.

- **Assessment of the external critique**: several of its points were sound and adopted --- (1) framing the bandit's claim correctly ("adaptively selects among strategies and learns which are associated with higher subsequent participation," not "predicts/proves the best intervention causally" -- this is standard bandit framing, matching how the project's own cited literature, e.g. Meyerhoff et al. 2023, already frames their own claims); (2) reducing arm count and folding "scripture" into a message-generation layer rather than its own arm, given the real population (~100-200) can't support 5 arms x 6 absence categories without severe sparsity; (3) not using member self-report as the reward for Bible Study specifically, since a member can trivially game "did you attend?" asked by the same bot that just delivered the intervention. One internal tension in the critique NOT resolved by it: it separately argues for richer context (more features) and for fewer arms/less sparsity, without noting these actually pull against each other -- flagged as a real design tension to watch, resolved via a context-generalizing bandit (see below) rather than a purely tabular one, deliberately chosen over the critique's own "daily fellowship" brainstorming, which turned out to be grounded in real DeKUTCU structure once checked (see next point) rather than needing to be taken on faith.
- **Real DeKUTCU activity structure, confirmed directly, not assumed**: Bible Study is Tuesdays specifically, and is the organization's own definition of "active member" (a strong institutional anchor for keeping it the primary evaluated activity). Monday/Wednesday(prayers)/Thursday/Friday have other, different fellowships -- open attendance, no fixed roster, unlike Bible Study's allocation-engine-formed groups. Sunday service stays broadcast-only per the proposal's own existing scope (1.6) -- untouched by any of this.
- **Settled design**: ONE shared bandit policy across all tracked weekday activities (Bible Study + the Mon/Wed/Thu/Fri fellowships), with which-activity-was-missed as a CONTEXT feature -- but the reward is always same-activity return, never cross-activity credit (missing Bible Study and later attending a Wednesday fellowship is never counted as the Bible-Study intervention working -- this was the critique's strongest, most concrete point, and the one design constraint held non-negotiable throughout). This turns 1 weekly learning opportunity per member into up to 5, without reintroducing the causal-attribution problem, since the underlying question the bandit actually answers ("given this absence context, which follow-up strategy works best") doesn't inherently require it to only ever be asked about Bible Study.
- **Two-tier data reliability, matched to what each tier is used for**: Bible Study attendance is confirmed by the member's own group LEADER (reusing Stage 5's leader-to-group matching directly -- no new mechanism needed) and is the PRIMARY, formally-evaluated metric -- the number defended without caveats in the viva. The open fellowships get a same-day WhatsApp check-in ("reply YES if you're here"), self-reported, with "regular attendee" status for each inferred dynamically from recent check-in history (e.g. present for 2 of the last 3 occurrences of that specific day) rather than a fixed roster -- this makes "absence" meaningful for an open gathering without changing its open character. This self-reported stream feeds the SAME shared policy as a secondary, lower-confidence data source (a standard, citable pattern -- weak supervision -- not something to hide in the write-up), never the primary evaluated claim.
- **Explicitly scoped OUT, with reasoning, rather than silently ignored**: discipleship classes (~10 attendance) -- too small a population for any bandit framing to learn anything meaningful; gets simple reminders via the already-built Stage 6 infrastructure instead, not ML. New believers never reaching new-believer's class -- a genuinely different problem (onboarding/intake funnel failure, not retention of an already-registered member), needing a separate intake pipeline this project's timeline doesn't support; to be named explicitly as a deliberate limitation in the final report.
- **The supervisor's broader "600 members, few leaders, inconsistent human follow-up" comment**: reframed rather than expanded into -- the project's existing `request_human`/`needs_support` intents (already real, not leader-gated) plus the not-yet-built Stage 11 (Escalation Manager) already constitute the right answer to this, once built and scoped org-wide rather than gated to Bible-Study placement. This maps directly onto the "stepped care" model from digital mental health literature (automated/self-service handling most contact, escalating to a human specifically when warranted) -- a citable, defensible frame for the discussion chapter, and a reason NOT to try to make the bandit itself solve a problem it was never meant to.
- **Not yet built** -- this entry records the settled design, not an implementation. Stage 7 (Reason Capture & Classifier) is the next concrete build step and needs its own design pass incorporating this: two distinct triggers (a leader marking a Bible Study absence; the system detecting a lapsed "regular" on an open fellowship) both funneling into the same reason-capture conversation.
